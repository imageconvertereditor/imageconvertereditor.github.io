JPG vs PNG vs WebP: Which Image Format Should You Use?

Photos, screenshots, logos and website images can all be saved in different formats. Choosing the right one keeps your pictures sharp and your files small. This guide explains the three most common formats in simple words, so you can pick the best one every time.

Quick answer

  • Photos from your camera: use JPG.
  • Logos, icons and screenshots with text: use PNG.
  • Images for a website: use WebP, or JPG if you need it to work everywhere.
  • Anything that needs a transparent background: use PNG or WebP.

What is JPG?

JPG (also written JPEG) is the most common photo format. It makes files small by throwing away a little detail that the human eye hardly notices. The more you compress a JPG, the smaller the file gets, but the softer the picture looks. JPG does not support transparent backgrounds, so any transparent area becomes a solid colour, usually white.

Good for: photos, social media pictures, form uploads, email attachments.

What is PNG?

PNG keeps every pixel exactly as it is, so nothing is lost when you save it again and again. It also supports transparent backgrounds. The price is a bigger file, especially for photos. PNG is at its best for flat colours, sharp edges and text.

Good for: logos, icons, screenshots, graphics with text, images that need a see-through background.

What is WebP?

WebP is a newer format made for the web. It can make files smaller than JPG and PNG at similar quality, and it supports transparency too. Modern browsers open it without trouble, but some older apps and gallery programs may not, so it is not always the best choice for sharing a file with other people.

Good for: images on websites where fast loading matters.

Side by side

  • File size: JPG is small, PNG is large for photos, WebP is usually the smallest.
  • Quality: JPG loses a little detail, PNG loses none, WebP can do either.
  • Transparent background: JPG no, PNG yes, WebP yes.
  • Works everywhere: JPG and PNG yes, WebP in modern browsers and most new apps.

Common mistakes

  • Saving a photo as PNG to "improve quality". Converting a JPG to PNG does not bring back lost detail. It only stops further loss.
  • Saving a logo as JPG. Edges can look fuzzy and the transparent background turns white.
  • Saving the same JPG again and again. Every save loses a little quality, so keep your original photo safe.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is JPG or PNG better quality?

PNG keeps all detail, so it is higher quality in a technical sense. For normal photos, a JPG at good quality looks almost the same and the file is much smaller.

Why is my PNG file so big?

PNG stores every pixel without throwing detail away, so photos in PNG can become very large. If you do not need transparency, convert the file to JPG.

Can every phone open WebP?

Most modern phones and browsers can. Some older apps and forms do not accept it, so use JPG when you are unsure.
